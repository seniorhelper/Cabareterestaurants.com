How to eat like you live here

Cabarete has a daily rhythm set by the wind and the sunset. Follow it and you will eat better.

The daily rhythm

  1. Morning: eat light. Start at a café. The wind usually picks up later, and you will want to be active, not heavy.
  2. Midday: lunch is the big meal for Dominicans. A comedor plate or fish on the beach.
  3. Sunset: the main event. Ask what came off the boat today at a beach restaurant such as La Casita de Papi, order that and watch the sky change over the water.
  4. Night: the bay gets lively. Beach bars play music, and pizza or tacos carry you through.

Local habits worth copying

  • Always carry cash. Many small places are cash only. Dominican pesos work everywhere; US dollars are often accepted at tourist spots at their rate.
  • Tuesday is Taco Tuesday at Gordito’s (our sponsor). Bring cash.
  • Ask for the catch, not the menu. "¿Qué pescado hay hoy?" works better than reading a laminated list.
  • Drink the fruit. Chinola, lechosa and guanábana juices, or a creamy morir soñando.
  • Check the bill. Tax and service are usually included; add a small cash tip for good service.

Where to go for a lively night

Mojito Bar and Pomodoro keep the beach busy in the evening, and Gordito’s is a quick taco stop. For a quieter, dressier dinner, book Bliss or Le Bistro. If you are in Sosúa, finish at the Waterfront over Playa Alicia.

Staying safe

Stick to busy, lit stretches of the beach at night, keep valuables light, and take a car or motoconcho for longer distances, agreeing on the fare first.
