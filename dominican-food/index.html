Food basics

Dominican food basics for Cabarete visitors

Cabarete’s menus are international, but the island’s own cooking is worth seeking out. Here is what to order and how to find it.

Dominican food is hearty, aromatic and built on plantain, rice, beans and slow-cooked meat, with seafood on the coast. It is rarely very spicy; flavor comes from sofrito, oregano, garlic and lime. In a town full of foreign-run restaurants, the Dominican dishes are often found at lunch counters and family kitchens rather than on the beachfront.

When to eat what

Mangú is a morning dish. La Bandera and sancocho are lunch: comedores cook in the morning and sell until the pots are empty, usually by mid-afternoon. Fried fish is any time at the beach. Sancocho is a Sunday and celebration favorite.

Ordering in Spanish

Many Cabarete restaurants have English menus, but at comedores and fruit stands a few words help: "un plato del día" (today’s plate), "con tostones" (with tostones), "sin hielo" (no ice), "¿cuánto cuesta?" (how much?) and "para llevar" (to go). Saying "buenos días" or "buenas tardes" before anything else is expected and appreciated.

Dominican dishes on Cabarete menus

On the beach strip, look for whole fried fish with tostones, shrimp in garlic or coconut sauce and seafood paella-style rice at the seafood restaurants. Breakfast cafés often offer a Dominican plate with mangú alongside waffles and bowls. For sancocho and La Bandera, the best bet is lunch at a Dominican family-run spot off the main road.

Market and colmado basics

If you have a kitchen, the local colmado (corner store) sells plantains, eggs, cheese, salami, rice, beans and cold drinks. Fruit trucks and stands sell what is in season. With those you can cook our sancocho and tostones.

Dominican food questions

What Dominican food should I try in Cabarete?

Start with sancocho, tostones, La Bandera and a mangú breakfast, plus whole fried fish by the water and fresh juices.

Where can I find Dominican food in Cabarete?

Beach seafood restaurants such as La Casita de Papi serve Dominican-style fish and sides. For sancocho and La Bandera, look for family-run places off the main strip, which serve them at lunch.

What fruit is in season?

Mangoes and avocados peak in summer, when fruit stands are full; passion fruit, papaya and coconuts are available most of the year.
