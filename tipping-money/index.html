Reading a bill in Cabarete

Beach towns can be confusing because pricing styles differ. Many sit-down restaurants list menu prices before charges, then add the 18% ITBIS sales tax and the 10% propina legal at the bottom: around 28% on top. Others, including some casual taco and beach spots, print prices with everything included. If the menu says "impuestos incluidos" or similar, untick the box above.

Example: dinner for three

Three friends spend RD$3,500 on food and drinks. Add the 10% service (RD$350) and 18% ITBIS (RD$630) and the bill reads RD$4,480. A 5% cash tip of RD$175 brings the total to RD$4,655, or about RD$1,552 each. Enter today’s exchange rate above to see it in US dollars.

The 10% and the extra tip

The 10% service charge is set by Article 228 of the Dominican Labor Code and is meant to be shared among staff. Because it is pooled, regulars often leave a little cash directly for their server. Reports say a court ruling restricts the 10% to dine-in orders, so takeout bills may not include it.

Cash, cards and dollars

  • Bring pesos for beach bars, taco counters, motoconchos and tips.
  • Cards work at many sit-down restaurants; ask first at small places.
  • US dollars are sometimes accepted, but the house exchange rate is usually worse than an ATM.

Questions about tipping

What are the extra charges on a Cabarete restaurant bill?

Usually two: an 18% ITBIS tax and a 10% legal service charge for staff, required by the Labor Code for dine-in service.

How much extra should I tip in Cabarete?

Nothing is required. A cash tip of about 5–10% handed to your server is a common thank-you for good service.
