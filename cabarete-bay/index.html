Eating along Cabarete Bay

The beach and the main road behind it hold most of Cabarete’s restaurants. Here is how to work the strip.

Cabarete town is essentially one long beach with a road running parallel behind it. Restaurants open straight onto the sand on one side and onto the road on the other, so you can walk the whole strip in about twenty minutes and pass dozens of places to eat. By day the bay is full of windsurfers and kiters; by evening tables move closer to the water and the strip turns social.

How the strip works

Beachfront restaurants charge a little more for the setting, and many are open from breakfast into the night. Places on the road side are often cheaper and more local. Walking between them on the sand at sunset is half the fun.

Where to go for what

  • Seafood with your toes in the sand: La Casita de Papi, famous for paella and shrimp a la Papi. Get there before sunset.
  • A celebration dinner: Bliss for Mediterranean, or Le Bistro for classic French.
  • Pizza, pasta and gelato: Pomodoro, right on the beach.
  • Drinks and all-day plates: Mojito Bar, no reservations.
  • Plant-based: Tuvà, recommended by Fodor’s.
  • Tacos: Gordito’s Fresh Mex at Plaza Ocean Dream (sponsored).

Practical tips

  • Cash: carry pesos. Some casual places are cash only.
  • Bills: expect tax and a service charge on sit-down bills. Our calculator helps.
  • Wind: afternoons are breezy on the sand. Bring a layer for dinner.
  • Getting back: motoconchos (motorbike taxis) and cars wait along the road; agree on the fare first.
