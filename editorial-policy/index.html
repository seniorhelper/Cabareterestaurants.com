Editorial policy and corrections

How we research, what we refuse to publish, and what we removed.

Our rules for research, wording and updates.

Sources first

A business is added only when we can find it in public sources we trust: its own site or active social account, official tourism material, an established guidebook or travel publisher, or a large listing platform. Each listing shows the sources and the month of our last check.

Our own words

We summarize what the sources report and say so ("a guidebook notes", "reviewers praise"). We do not copy reviews, and we do not publish star scores or review totals.

Facts we leave out

Opening hours, prices and phone numbers appear only when the business publishes them. Otherwise we tell you to check, because these change constantly.

How tags work

Labels like "breakfast", "on the beach" or "date night" power our planner. They reflect what the cited sources describe and are the editor's call, never something a business pays for.

Pictures

Generic food photography illustrates a cuisine or occasion and is never presented as a specific restaurant's food.

Locations

We pin a place on a map only with a coordinate checked against OpenStreetMap or an official geocoder; otherwise we link to a map search.

Keeping it current

Every listing is re-checked each quarter. Closures come off the list and are recorded below.

Recent removals and corrections

We keep this log so readers and search engines can see what changed and why.

  • Star ratings and review counts: Removed from every card (for example "4.3 · 1,068"). They had no source or date. Each listing now links to current reviews instead.
  • Rating and review markup for Gordito’s Fresh Mex: Removed the AggregateRating (4.7/1333) and Review structured data. Search engines do not allow review markup a site did not collect itself. Gordito’s is now clearly labeled Sponsored.
  • "Your Restaurant Here" placeholders: Removed empty advertising slots.
