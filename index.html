Quick answer: what is the food scene like in Cabarete?

Varied and walkable. Restaurants line the beach of Cabarete Bay and the main road behind it, with more spots at Kite Beach to the west. Decades of visitors and settlers from abroad mean you will find Italian, French, Mexican and plant-based kitchens next to Dominican seafood and comedores. Breakfast cafés open early for kiters; beach restaurants come alive at sunset. Sosúa, a short drive west, adds another cluster of dinner spots.

Why Cabarete eats different

The secret behind the food scene

Most Caribbean beach towns offer resort buffets or street carts. Cabarete grew differently: kitesurfers, windsurfers and other long-stay visitors arrived from Europe and North America and some opened the kind of places they missed from home. Italian kitchens, a French bistro and a Mexican taco counter now sit beside Dominican beach grills supplied by local fishermen.

The result is a short beachfront strip with real variety. You can eat Italian for lunch and Dominican seafood for dinner within a few minutes’ walk. Read how to eat like you live here.

Cabarete restaurants FAQ

What are the best restaurants in Cabarete?

For beach seafood, La Casita de Papi is the long-standing favorite. For a special dinner, Fodor’s highlights Bliss, and Le Bistro is the French option. Breakfast regulars go to Cabarete Coffee Company and Vagamundo, Tuvà is the plant-based pick, and Kite Beach has La Mesa Taína and Chichigua. Gordito’s Fresh Mex is our sponsored partner for tacos. See every listing with sources.

What should I eat in Cabarete?

Fresh fish and seafood on the bay, paella at La Casita de Papi, tacos, Dominican staples such as sancocho, tostones and La Bandera, and fruit smoothies everywhere. Our Dominican food basics page explains the local dishes.

Are Cabarete restaurants expensive?

There is a wide spread. Beach bars, taco spots and cafés are the cheapest, the beachfront seafood places are mid-range, and dinner-only restaurants such as Bliss or Le Bistro cost the most. Sit-down bills usually add 18% tax and 10% service; the bill calculator shows the total.

Do Cabarete restaurants take cards?

Some casual places are cash only; Gordito’s says so on its menu, and a guide notes La Casita de Papi takes cash or Mastercard. Higher-end restaurants usually take cards. Carry pesos for small places and tips.

Is there vegetarian or vegan food in Cabarete?

Yes. Fodor’s recommends Tuvà for plant-based dining, Fresh Fresh focuses on healthy bowls and juices, and cafés such as Vagamundo have smoothie bowls. Gordito’s menu lists bean and veggie tacos, burritos and bowls.

When is the best time to eat in Cabarete?

Eat breakfast early, before the wind picks up and the beach fills with kites. Lunch runs roughly noon to 2 p.m. For dinner, aim for sunset on the bay. Beach bars stay open late.

Where should I eat in Sosúa?

Sosúa, a short drive west, has the Waterfront restaurant over Playa Alicia for sunset, Casa Veintiuno for a polished dinner and Mosha’s for Jamaican food. Read the Sosúa guide.

Are there good restaurants near Puerto Plata?

Yes. Puerto Plata, west along the coast past Sosúa, has Malecón seafood and restaurants in Victorian houses. Our sister site Puerto Plata Restaurants covers it.

Do I need to tip at restaurants in Cabarete?

Bills normally include 18% ITBIS tax and a 10% legal service charge shared among staff. Many diners add 5–10% in cash for their server when service is good. Some beachfront places quote prices with everything included, so check the menu.

Is the water and ice safe in Cabarete restaurants?

Locals and visitors drink bottled or purified water rather than tap water. Established restaurants and bars serve purified water and use ice made from it. At small roadside stands, a sealed bottled drink is the simplest choice.

Is lobster always available?

No. The Dominican Republic has an annual closed season (veda) for spiny lobster to protect breeding, and fresh local lobster should not be on menus then. Outside the veda it is a popular splurge.

How do I get to Cabarete from Puerto Plata airport?

Gregorio Luperón International Airport (POP) is about 40 minutes away by taxi or shuttle along the coast road. Arrange a transfer in advance if you land late.
