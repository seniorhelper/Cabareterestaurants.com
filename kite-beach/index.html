Kite Beach: where to eat between sessions

West of town, Kite Beach is where the kites fill the sky. A few waterfront kitchens feed the riders and the people watching them.

Kite Beach sits at the western end of Cabarete, a short ride or a long beach walk from the town center. The steady trade winds make it one of the best-known kiteboarding spots in the Caribbean, and the beach is lined with kite schools, small hotels and places to eat that cater to people coming straight off the water.

Eating here

Food at Kite Beach is about good fuel and a view of the action. Expect big breakfasts, burgers, fish, poke-style bowls and fresh juices, plus cold drinks once the wind drops. Lunch is the busiest meal, between sessions.

  • La Mesa Taína serves breakfast, lunch and dinner on the waterfront from a wide menu with sushi, tapas and burgers.
  • Chichigua is a kiter and surfer hangout; reviewers like the dorado.

Tips for riders and spectators

  • Eat light before you ride and save the big plate for after.
  • Bring sun protection; shade is limited on the sand at midday.
  • Most Kite Beach spots are casual: swimwear and a cover-up are fine.
  • For dinner choice, head back into town to the bay strip, about five minutes by car.

A Kite Beach day

Start with breakfast in town or at the beach before the wind fills in. Ride or watch through the late morning, then take a long lunch at a waterfront table while the kites keep flying. In the late afternoon, when the wind eases, head back toward the bay for a sunset drink and dinner on the strip. Remember water and sunscreen: the breeze hides how strong the sun is.

Beyond Kite Beach

Further west, Encuentro Beach is known for surfing and has a few simple shacks; we will list specific places there once we can verify them. Plan your day with the food planner.
